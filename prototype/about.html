About Us

The Felidae Conservation Fund provides leadership for world-class research studies. We also provide strategy, planning, funding, media, field support, and logistics. We get to work with partners all over the world! We provide them with the best research and studies on human’s impact on wild cats and how we can live with them.

The organization was born when our founder had her first experience with a puma in the wild. The encounter was very up close. It left her feeling like an intruder in the natural space. From there, it became apparent that there was a need for forward-thinking wild cat research in the conservation space. Only through this research and education will we truly be able to heal people’s relationships with wild cats and live in harmony.

Our Impact

We work every day to help people feel safer in their homes near wild cats. We help them feel confident to responsibly adventure into nature! Our goal is to protect wild cats’ ecosystems and encourage healthy relationships with them. We’ve also been able to educate over 30,000 people at events and rallies. This has resulted in influencing over 4,750 people to take conservation action!

That’s nearly 5,000 people working with us to encourage wild cat conservation. The best news yet is that the number continues to grow every day—and you can be one of them.

In addition, since 2018 we have...

  • Worked with over 3,100 participants on field trips
  • Helped more than 8,200 homeowners feel safer in their neighborhoods

Our Mission

We firmly believe that we can live in harmony with nature. Our mission is to improve the state of global wild cat ecosystems. We work through a fusion of research, education, and online technologies. Our end goal will benefit humanity and drive meaningful change in the natural world.

Our work allows us to get involved in the classroom and the field. We work with the public to educate them on how best to interact with and support wild cat conservation. We are also committed to maintaining the most up-to-date and innovative research.

Who We Are

Maintaining local ecosystems and educating people on wild cats is the cornerstone of wild cat conservation. We work with an incredible team of volunteers to do this. They help us conduct field work, research, and education events.

We also have a highly qualified board of directors and staff. They make the decisions on where and how we conduct our forward-thinking research in wild cat conservation. Our board also strategizes how to best use our resources and funds.
