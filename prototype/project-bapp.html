Bay Area Puma Project

Non-invasive puma research and conservation across the San Francisco Bay Area — the first large-scale study of the region's mountain lions, and the outreach that turns its findings into protected habitat.

Why this project exists

The Bay Area Puma Project (BAPP) is the first large-scale research, education and conservation program for pumas in and around the San Francisco Bay Area. Its primary goal is to increase knowledge, understanding and awareness about Bay Area puma populations, in order to foster a more harmonious relationship between people and the natural world.

As the top predator in the natural spaces around the Bay, the puma plays a critical role in maintaining the health and balance of local ecosystems. But human development is rapidly encroaching on puma habitat, bringing habitat fragmentation and corridor loss, more anxiety in communities where pumas are seen, and more conflict involving roads, livestock and depredation.

Because the puma is both a bellwether and a keystone species, those pressures threaten biodiversity and the wider habitat — not just one cat.

How the work fits together

A cutting-edge field study provides the scientific foundation, revealing new data on puma biology and behavior. That knowledge supports community outreach and education programs that raise local awareness of why these ecosystems matter, backed by online tools that reach a far broader public.

The detailed data the research generates then does its most important job: it advances collaborative discussions with the agencies and officials who can safeguard key wildlife habitats and corridors.

Objectives

Three lines of work, one outcome

Field research

  • Collect data on puma movements, biology, ecology and behavior
  • Analyze that data and develop actionable steps for conservation
  • Publish documents that support conservation and add to scientific knowledge
  • Run extensive remote-camera research to help determine density
  • Collect biological samples for health and genetic testing

Outreach & education

  • Inform local residents, reduce tensions, increase understanding
  • Give students a deeper appreciation for their local ecosystems
  • Connect people to nature using the latest technologies

Conservation

  • Secure protection for critical wildlife linkages
  • Secure protection for priority habitats
  • Minimize human-puma encounters through better land-use policy
